## Method as implemented

Detection (replay "events"):

- Signal: sorted units, restricted to the probe-sequence cells for one running direction. "Cells with a peak firing rate of at least 5 Hz were included in the probe sequence, with the exception of putative inhibitory interneurons, which were identified as cells with a mean peak-to-trough spike width of less than 0.35 ms." Place fields were "velocity-filtered to exclude times when the speed of the animal was below 5.4 cm s−1" (Methods, "Place-field analysis", p. 682). 17–18 tetrodes in CA1 (Methods, p. 682).

- Behavioral domain: "A spike train was constituted from all spikes (from all cells in the probe sequence) that occurred during stopping periods while the animal faced in the direction in which it had just run." (Methods, "Spike-train analysis", p. 682). "Stopping periods" are not defined quantitatively in the main text or in the Supplementary Information. They are described only behaviourally: "Within a given lap, the animal stopped at each end to consume food ... would wait of its own accord in the same position for a short period of time" (p. 680). Direction was measured "by the relative position of two tracker diodes" (p. 682).

- Segmentation: "This spike train was then broken between every pair of successive spikes separated by more than 50 ms, to form a large set of proto-events." (Methods, pp. 682–683). Unlike Lee & Wilson 2002, there is no per-cell burst collapse. Every spike of every probe cell enters the pooled train.

- Participation: "Those proto-events in which at least one-third of the cells in the probe sequence fired at least one spike were then selected as events." (Methods, p. 683).

- Maximum duration: "The few events longer than 500 ms in duration were rejected as a potential source of spurious correlations." (Methods, p. 683). Duration is presumably first to last spike (inference; not defined).

- Minimum duration, smoothing, normalization, LFP criterion: none stated.

- Sleep: not applicable (awake stopping periods). A separate control used pre-running rest periods, but those were not detected differently.

Separate ripple detection (analysis only, Fig. 4c cross-correlogram):

- "Ripples were identified as reported previously8. A single time of occurrence for each ripple was calculated as the mean of the start and end times of the ripple." (Methods, "Ripple identification", p. 683). Ref. 8 is Lee & Wilson 2002. The paper also notes: "Sharp waves reverse at about the electrode depth corresponding to maximum cell yield in the hippocampus, making it difficult to measure sharp waves directly. However, they co-occur with ... ripples (100–400 Hz)."

## Inherited from

- Ripple detection: Lee & Wilson 2002 ([paper note](54_Lee_2002.md)). The 100–400 Hz filter, |filtered| > mean + 5 SD over the reference period, crossings grouped at ≤ 20 ms, intervals < 20 ms rejected. Lee used "all of SWS" as the reference period, and Foster does not say what the reference period is for awake data.

- The spike-train parsing is a variant of Lee & Wilson 2002, with a 50 ms gap and no max_isi step. It is described fully here, so I followed no further citation.
