# Foster 2006 — Reverse replay of behavioural sequences in hippocampal place cells during the awake state
Source: the extracted text (pdftotext of the Dropbox PDF). I also checked the Supplementary Information from nature.com (MOESM1 PDF of figures, MOESM2 guide), saved under scratchpad/papers/foster2006_supp/. Title verified: yes (Foster & Wilson, Nature 440:680–683).
Trigger: other (gap segmentation of the pooled spike train of sorted template cells, plus a participation criterion)

## Method as implemented

Detection (replay "events"):
- Signal: sorted units, restricted to the probe-sequence cells for one running direction. "Cells with a peak firing rate of at least 5 Hz were included in the probe sequence, with the exception of putative inhibitory interneurons, which were identified as cells with a mean peak-to-trough spike width of less than 0.35 ms." Place fields were "velocity-filtered to exclude times when the speed of the animal was below 5.4 cm s−1" (Methods, "Place-field analysis", p. 682). 17–18 tetrodes in CA1 (Methods, p. 682).
- Behavioral domain: "A spike train was constituted from all spikes (from all cells in the probe sequence) that occurred during stopping periods while the animal faced in the direction in which it had just run." (Methods, "Spike-train analysis", p. 682). "Stopping periods" are not defined quantitatively in the main text or in the Supplementary Information. They are described only behaviourally: "Within a given lap, the animal stopped at each end to consume food ... would wait of its own accord in the same position for a short period of time" (p. 680). Direction was measured "by the relative position of two tracker diodes" (p. 682).
- Segmentation: "This spike train was then broken between every pair of successive spikes separated by more than 50 ms, to form a large set of proto-events." (Methods, pp. 682–683). Unlike Lee & Wilson 2002, there is no per-cell burst collapse. Every spike of every probe cell enters the pooled train.
- Participation: "Those proto-events in which at least one-third of the cells in the probe sequence fired at least one spike were then selected as events." (Methods, p. 683).
- Maximum duration: "The few events longer than 500 ms in duration were rejected as a potential source of spurious correlations." (Methods, p. 683). Duration is presumably first to last spike (inference; not defined).
- Minimum duration, smoothing, normalization, LFP criterion: none stated.
- Sleep: not applicable (awake stopping periods). A separate control used pre-running rest periods, but those were not detected differently.

Separate ripple detection (analysis only, Fig. 4c cross-correlogram):
- "Ripples were identified as reported previously8. A single time of occurrence for each ripple was calculated as the mean of the start and end times of the ripple." (Methods, "Ripple identification", p. 683). Ref. 8 is Lee & Wilson 2002. The paper also notes: "Sharp waves reverse at about the electrode depth corresponding to maximum cell yield in the hippocampus, making it difficult to measure sharp waves directly. However, they co-occur with ... ripples (100–400 Hz)."

## Inherited from
- Ripple detection: Lee & Wilson 2002 (manifest 54_Lee_2002). The 100–400 Hz filter, |filtered| > mean + 5 SD over the reference period, crossings grouped at ≤ 20 ms, intervals < 20 ms rejected. Lee used "all of SWS" as the reference period, and Foster does not say what the reference period is for awake data.
- The spike-train parsing is a variant of Lee & Wilson 2002, with a 50 ms gap and no max_isi step. It is described fully here, so I followed no further citation.

## Survey CSV discrepancies
Row 53:
- Detection "MUA": the paper uses sorted probe-sequence place cells (peak ≥ 5 Hz, interneurons excluded), not multiunit.
- Animal Speed "#N/A": detection is restricted to "stopping periods while the animal faced in the direction in which it had just run". No numeric speed is given, but it is a behavioural detection criterion that the row does not record. The 5.4 cm/s value is for place fields only.
- Combine Events Thresh. "#N/A": the 50 ms value is a split gap (a gap > 50 ms separates events, a gap ≤ 50 ms joins them), so one could enter 50 here. The Detection Notes record it.
- Min. Cells 33% and Max Duration 500 match.
Count: 1 real discrepancy (MUA vs sorted units), plus 1 omission (stopping-period and facing-direction restriction).

Recipe:
```python
import numpy as np
from ripple_detection import merge_close_events

# spikes: list of spike-time arrays for the probe-sequence cells of one direction,
# already restricted to user-defined stopping periods while facing the direction just run
st = np.concatenate(spikes)
su = np.concatenate([np.full(s.size, i) for i, s in enumerate(spikes)])
order = np.argsort(st, kind="stable"); st, su = st[order], su[order]
proto = merge_close_events(np.column_stack([st, st]), close_event_threshold=0.050 + 1e-9)
n_cells = np.array([np.unique(su[(st >= a) & (st <= b)]).size for a, b in proto])
duration = proto[:, 1] - proto[:, 0]
events = proto[(n_cells >= np.ceil(len(spikes) / 3)) & (duration <= 0.500)]
```
Smoke-tested on simulated spike trains. It runs (238 proto-events, then the 1/3 and 500 ms rules).
Remaining deviations:
- As with Lee 2002, the package contributes only merge_close_events. The per-event distinct-cell count and the 1/3 rule are user numpy, because multiunit_HSE_detector's n_active_units applies to its own z-scored events, not to gap-defined ones.
- Strict vs non-strict gap: merge_close_events joins gaps < threshold, and the paper breaks gaps > 50 ms (so it joins gaps ≤ 50 ms). Hence the `+ 1e-9`.
- "One-third of the cells": ≥ n/3 read as ceil(n/3). The paper does not specify rounding.
- Stopping periods and facing direction must be supplied by the user. The paper gives no speed threshold, and no facing-direction definition beyond the diode geometry. Whether a proto-event must lie wholly inside a stopping period is moot here, because the spike train is built only from stopping-period spikes. That means the train breaks at the edges of stopping periods.
- Duration: first to last spike, assumed.
Smallest package addition (if C): not needed for B. To make it A, the same spike-time gap segmenter as Lee 2002 would do, `segment_spike_train(spike_times, unit_ids, max_gap, max_isi=None)` returning n_distinct_units, plus a duration filter.
