## Method as implemented

Detection (candidate events). Quotes are from Materials and Methods, "Candidate events and trajectory-specific subregions", p. 6460, unless noted:
- Signal: sorted putative place cells. "a smoothed spike density function was computed with all spikes from all putative place cells (10 ms time bins; Gaussian filter SD = 15 ms)."
- Unit set: "Putative interneurons (mean peak-to-trough spike width <0.34 ms) and neurons with insignificant place fields (peak firing rate on the Y maze ≤1 Hz) were excluded ... The rest of the single units were considered putative place cells (88 from Rat 1, 67 from Rat 2, 58 from Rat 3)" (p. 6460).
- Normalization: "For each recording session, a smoothed spike density function was computed". The mean and SD are "of the function", with no restriction to a behavioural state. Inference: statistics over the whole session.
- Threshold and bounds: "Candidate events were defined as epochs of spikes during which spike densities were above the mean of the function, and contained peaks above 2 SDs over the mean." So the peak must exceed 2 SD and the bounds are the crossings of the mean.
- Speed: "Only candidate events that occurred when a rat's speed was <5 cm/s were considered (2514 from Rat 1, 3222 from Rat 2, 2902 from Rat 3)." How "occurred when" is tested (peak, endpoints or whole event) is not stated.
- Location: Results say "candidate events ... occurring during stopping periods restricted to the three reward areas" (p. 6463). Stopping periods are "periods of time spent between runs at a reward area" (Methods, "Cumulative replay numbers", p. 6461).
- Minimum and maximum duration for candidates: not stated. The mean was 154.6 ms (Results, p. 6463).
- Merging of close events: not stated. No participation minimum. No brain-state criterion.

Later analysis restrictions (not detection):
- Replay subregions: "(1) length ≥50 ms (five time bins); (2) arm coverage of >50%; and (3) absolute weighted correlation > 0.5" (Methods, p. 6461). These criteria apply to decoded trajectory-specific subregions.
- Ripple analysis (ripple counts within replays; ripple amplitude at the choice point): "Ripple amplitude was calculated as in Davidson et al. (2009), with minor changes. We filtered the LFP signal from each selected channel in the ripple band (150–250 Hz), and Hilbert transformed the filtered signal to compute its envelope as the absolute value of the transformation. The mean envelope averaged across all selected tetrodes (15, 13, and 13 of 19 tetrodes for Rats 1, 2, and 3) was smoothed with a Gaussian window (SD = 8 ms) to represent a continuous mean ripple amplitude. Individual ripples were also detected as local peaks in the ripple amplitude curve >2.5 SDs above the mean, which were both calculated across all stopping periods." (Methods, "Ripple and multiunit activity analyses", p. 6463). This is the mean envelope across tetrodes, then Gaussian smoothing; each local peak is a ripple.
- MUA display and analysis: "Multiunit spikes were defined as all recorded spikes whose largest amplitudes across tetrode channels were >100 μV. Multiunit spike density was smoothed across 10 ms bins (Gaussian filter; SD = 6 ms)" (p. 6463). This is not used for detection.

```python
import numpy as np, pandas as pd
from ripple_detection import multiunit_HSE_detector, require_overlap, segment_boolean_series
# counts10: (n_bins, n_place_cells) spike counts in 10 ms bins; t10, speed10 on the same 100 Hz grid
cand = multiunit_HSE_detector(
    t10, counts10, speed10, 100.0,
    smoothing_sigma=0.015, zscore_threshold=2.0, minimum_duration=0.0,  # peak > 2 SD, bounds at mean
    normalization_mask=None,                                            # whole-session statistics
    speed_threshold=5.0,
)
# location restriction from Results: keep events during stops at a reward area
at_reward = np.array(segment_boolean_series(pd.Series(in_reward_area & (speed10 < 5), index=t10), 0.0))
cand = require_overlap(cand, at_reward)
```
Smoke-tested at 100 Hz on binned simulate_session counts. The package has no minimum sampling-rate check, so 10 ms bins work.
Remaining deviations:
- Speed rule: the package tests speed at the first and last sample. The paper's "occurred when a rat's speed was <5 cm/s" is unspecified, and the inequality is < rather than ≤.
- Reward-area restriction: the user must build it from position. require_overlap keeps an event that overlaps a reward-area stop at all, which may be looser than "occurring during".
- Event bounds are quantized to 10 ms bins, as in the paper. The Gaussian kernel is only 1.5 bins wide at this rate, which is also true of the paper.
- Threshold inequalities: "above 2 SDs" (strict) versus ≥, and "above the mean" versus z ≥ 0.
