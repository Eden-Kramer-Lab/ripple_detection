## Method as implemented

Detection (population burst events, PBEs):

- "We detected PBEs by the multiunit activity (MUA) as in previous studies (Diba and Buzsaki, 2007; Wu et al., 2017). In each session, all putative spikes recorded by all tetrodes in the CA1 were binned and counted in each 10 ms time bin. Spike counts in each bin were smoothed by a Gaussian kernel with a σ of two bins and normalized from 0 to 1. A PBE was defined as a time period within which the normalized peak MUA spike counts exceeded a threshold of 0.35, with its start and end times detected by crossing a threshold of 0.15. Adjacent events with gap < 30 ms were combined." (STAR Methods, "LFP analysis and population burst events (PBEs)").

- Signal: all putative spikes, unsorted. "A 60 μV threshold was set for spike detection" (STAR Methods, "Data acquisition"). Tetrodes are in the CA1 pyramidal layer ("Recording procedure").

- Smoothing: Gaussian σ = 2 bins = 20 ms, on 10 ms bins.

- Normalization: "normalized from 0 to 1", i.e. a range normalization, not a z-score. Whether this is min–max or division by the maximum is not stated, but for a spike-count trace with minimum ≈ 0 the two nearly coincide. The period is "In each session".

- Thresholds: peak "exceeded" 0.35 (strict), and bounds are the crossings of 0.15.

- Merging: "Adjacent events with gap < 30 ms were combined."

- Minimum and maximum duration: not stated.

- Speed: none stated for detection. The analysed PBEs are those "within the water consumption periods in the observation box" (STAR Methods, "Replay identification and shuffling": "a PBE rate (number of PBEs per s within the water consumption periods in the observation box)"). Results: "Ripples and PBEs primarily took place during reward consumption when the OB was stationary with low speeds". The restriction to reward-consumption periods is an analysis window, not a speed threshold. How consumption periods are defined is not stated beyond the task description.

- Brain state: none (awake task).

- Participation (analysis, not detection): "For each template, we defined those PBEs with at least 4 active template cells as candidate events." (STAR Methods, "Replay identification and shuffling").

- LFP: ripple power (PSD 100–250 Hz, compared over 130–220 Hz) was used only to compare conditions, not for detection. LFPs were "not always available because of the noise produced by animals making nosepokes".

- Separately, "stopping periods (velocity < 5 cm/s for > 3 s)" were excluded from place-field rate curves ("Firing rate curves and template construction"). That is analysis only.

## Inherited from

The paper cites Diba & Buzsáki 2007 and Wu et al. 2017 "as in previous studies", but it states a different, complete rule. Wu 2017 ([paper note](33_Wu_2017.md)) uses unsmoothed 10 ms counts, peak ≥ mean + 4 SD, bounds at the mean and 50–400 ms, with no range normalization and no merge. So "as in previous studies" refers to using MUA, not to the thresholds. Nothing needed to be followed to reproduce it.

## Code

"The main MATLAB codes for the analysis are publicly available in GitHub (https://github.com/DaoyunJiLab/DM2021.git; DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.5758889)." I downloaded the repository (commit c7327f8) and grepped for the MUA/PBE detection (0.35, 0.15, MUA, burst). The PBE detection is not in the repo. It has the DataManager GUI, place-field and decoding code, and a menu entry naming `DataManager_V1CA1_FindMUAlevel_Callback`, whose file is absent. So the code does not settle the min–max vs divide-by-max question.

## Analysis and interpretation

Only used non-empty time bins

## Uncertainties

The referenced `DataManager_V1CA1_FindMUAlevel_Callback` is absent from the release. Original population-rate normalization (min–max versus division by maximum) remains unknown.

## Package mapping

Packaged primary method: `mou_2022` in [literature_methods.py](../../../src/ripple_detection/literature_methods.py). Its docstring records implementation choices and assumptions. Simulation checks establish that it runs; they do not establish equivalence to the authors’ original event set.

The method defaults to `stage="detection"`. Use `stage="decoding_candidates"` for the analysis participation and duration restrictions described above.
