# Lee 2002 — Memory of Sequential Experience in the Hippocampus during Slow Wave Sleep
Source: the extracted text (pdftotext of the Dropbox PDF); title verified: yes (Lee & Wilson, Neuron 36:1183–1194). pdftotext renders "=" as "⫽"; quotes below restore "=".
Trigger: other (spike-train gap segmentation of sorted units; no threshold on any trace)

## Method as implemented

Detection (the "words", i.e. SWS population bursts):
- Signal: sorted single units, CA1 pyramidal cells only, and only the cells in one RUN place-field sequence at a time. "We parsed the SWS activity of the cells from the POS and NEG place field sequences separately—the SWS activity of the cells active in the POS behavioral sequence gave one set of words ... and those from NEG another set" (Results, p. 1184). Cells were clustered with XCLUST from 4–8 tetrodes (Experimental Procedures, "Electrophysiology", p. 1192). So the events depend on which template's cells are used. They are not multiunit.
- Step 1, per-cell burst collapse (max_isi): "for each cell, each set of consecutive spikes with interspike intervals less than max_isi (ms) was grouped into a single event (represented by a single letter identifying that cell) occurring at the time of the first spike. Each isolated spike (i.e. a spike separated by at least max_isi from any other spike of that cell) also produced a letter." (Results, p. 1184).
- Step 2, gap segmentation across cells (max_gap): "the letters from all the cells were merged to form a single, time-ordered string. This string was then broken between every pair of adjacent letters separated by more than max_gap (ms) into a set of shorter strings (words). Each word thus represents the relative order of cell activity within an SWS population burst." (Results, p. 1184). The gap is measured between letter times, i.e. between the first spikes of per-cell bursts, not from the end of the previous burst.
- Parameter values: the analysis was swept over max_isi and max_gap, "with the constraint that max_isi ≤ max_gap". The best values were "max_isi = 50 ms and max_gap = 100 ms ... These values parse the activity into words such that the resulting mean interword interval is 804 ms, a number consistent with the hippocampal EEG sharp-wave/ripple occurrence rate of approximately 0.5–1 Hz" (Results, p. 1186). Table 1 note: "All of the pair (PA), triplet (T), and low-probability (LP) trial results are for the case of max_isi = 50 ms and max_gap = 100 ms" (p. 1185).
- No threshold, no smoothing, no normalization, no LFP criterion, no minimum or maximum duration: not stated anywhere for the words. Every word, however short, is kept, and words are then grouped by content.
- Participation (analysis, not detection): words are classified as "pairs, two-letter words with two distinct letters; triplets, three-letter words with three distinct letters; and low-probability trials ... generally consisting of words with four or more distinct letters as well as any additional repeated letters" (Results, p. 1184).
- Duration (reported, not a criterion): "Figure 6A shows the distribution of durations (time between first and last letters) of all PRE and POST low-probability trials (mean 154 ± 84 ms)" (Results, p. 1188).
- Brain state (detection domain): SWS only. "Sleep, identified based on visual assessment (through videotape) of rat posture during PRE and POST, was partitioned into SWS and REM as follows. REM was identified as periods with an elevated ratio (averaged every 1 s) of hippocampal EEG power in the theta band (5–12 Hz) to overall power (1–475 Hz). The remainder was classified as SWS. All SWS from PRE and POST was used in our analyses." (Experimental Procedures, "Identification of SWS", p. 1192). The REM ratio threshold is not stated.
- Speed: none. The animals were asleep in a box.

Separate ripple detection (used only for Fig. 6B/C, the timing of ripples relative to words; not the trigger):
- "Hippocampal EEG was filtered in the ripple band (100–400 Hz), the mean and standard deviation of the absolute value of this filtered trace for all of SWS was computed, and a threshold was set to five standard deviations above the mean. Threshold crossings (times when the absolute value of the filtered trace was above threshold) with interevent interval ≤20 ms were grouped together to form a single interval. Intervals <20 ms in duration were rejected, and the rest were classified as ripples. Threshold was verified by visual inspection" (Experimental Procedures, "Identification of Ripples", p. 1193).
- The measure is the rectified filtered signal (|x|), not an envelope. Statistics come from all of SWS. There is no extension to the mean: the bounds are the 5 SD crossings. The filter type and which tetrode's EEG was used are not stated ("EEG ... was recorded from each tetrode", p. 1192). The EEG was sampled at 1.5 kHz.

## Survey CSV discrepancies
Row 54:
- Detection "MUA": the paper uses sorted single units (template cells, per direction), not multiunit. Collapsing each cell's bursts into one letter requires cell identity.
- Combine Events Thresh. 100: the value is correct, but it is a split rule ("broken between every pair of adjacent letters separated by more than max_gap"), so a gap of ≤ 100 ms joins. It is applied between letters (first spikes of per-cell bursts), not between detected events. The Detection Notes record this correctly.
- The SWR fields are #N/A. That is acceptable for the trigger. The paper does have a ripple detector (100–400 Hz, |filtered| > mean + 5 SD over SWS, grouping ≤ 20 ms, minimum 20 ms), but it is used only for the ripple-timing analysis.
- The Detection Notes are otherwise accurate: max_isi 50 ms, max_gap 100 ms, theta (5–12 Hz) to total power ratio for REM, visual inspection of state.
Count: 1 real discrepancy (MUA vs sorted units), plus 1 clarification (the combine value is a split gap).

## Package mapping

Recipe:
```python
import numpy as np
from ripple_detection import merge_close_events

def letters(spike_times, max_isi=0.050):
    """First spike of each run of spikes with ISI < max_isi (one cell)."""
    new = np.concatenate([[True], np.diff(spike_times) >= max_isi])
    return spike_times[new]

# spikes: list of 1-D arrays of spike times (s) for the template cells, restricted to SWS
lt = np.concatenate([letters(s) for s in spikes])
lu = np.concatenate([np.full(letters(s).size, i) for i, s in enumerate(spikes)])
order = np.argsort(lt, kind="stable"); lt, lu = lt[order], lu[order]
# a split at gaps > max_gap is a merge of zero-length intervals at gaps <= max_gap
words = merge_close_events(np.column_stack([lt, lt]), close_event_threshold=0.100 + 1e-9)
n_distinct = np.array([np.unique(lu[(lt >= a) & (lt <= b)]).size for a, b in words])
low_probability_trials = words[n_distinct >= 4]            # analysis grouping
# SWS restriction: build SWS intervals yourself (user-computed theta/total ratio + video) and
# filter spike times to SWS BEFORE parsing. exclude_overlap(words, non_sws) afterwards would keep
# single-letter (zero-length) words, because a zero-length interval never overlaps anything in _overlaps.
```
Smoke-tested on simulate_multiunit output converted to spike times. merge_close_events on zero-length intervals gives the same word count as a direct `np.split(lt, np.flatnonzero(np.diff(lt) > 0.1) + 1)` (184 = 184 words on sparse simulated firing).
Remaining deviations:
- Package contribution is thin: merge_close_events does the gap split. The per-cell burst collapse (max_isi) and the distinct-letter count are user numpy. The package has no spike-time API. Every detector takes binned arrays.
- merge_close_events merges gaps strictly below the threshold, and the paper joins gaps ≤ max_gap. Hence the `+ 1e-9`. Spike times have 0.1 ms precision, so an exact tie is possible but rare.
- The events depend on the chosen cell set (POS vs NEG template cells). With unsorted multiunit the max_isi step is undefined, because a per-cell burst cannot be collapsed without cell identity.
- SWS gating: filtering spikes to SWS before parsing lets a word join letters across a non-SWS gap of ≤ 100 ms (rare with long SWS epochs). The paper does not say how word parsing treats SWS boundaries.
- The package has no sleep scoring. The theta (5–12 Hz) to 1–475 Hz power ratio at 1 s resolution and the video posture assessment are user code. The REM ratio threshold is not stated in the paper.
- The secondary ripple detector is B on its own: `filter_ripple_band(lfp, 1500, band=(100, 400))`, `np.abs`, `normalize_signal(..., normalization_mask=sws)`, `segment_boolean_series(z > 5, 0.0)`, `merge_close_events(..., 0.020 + eps)`, then keep duration ≥ 0.020. Deviations: FIR remez vs the unstated filter; rectified signal, not an envelope.
Smallest package addition (if C): not needed for B. To make it A, add a spike-time gap segmenter, e.g. `segment_spike_train(spike_times, unit_ids, max_gap, max_isi=None) -> DataFrame[start_time, end_time, n_spikes, n_distinct_units]`, which would also cover Foster 2006.
