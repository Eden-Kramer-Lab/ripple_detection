## Method as implemented
Replay events are defined by decoding, not by an LFP or MUA candidate detector:
- Immobility: "The number of immobility periods was computed by counting the number of periods the animal had a speed of lower than 5 cm/s" (Behavioral Correlates, p. e5). For rate maps, "running (speed > 5 cm/s) and immobility (speed < 5 cm/s) periods" (Cross-validation, p. e3).
- Every immobility period is scanned with decoding windows: "Each immobility period was divided into spiking-windows and, using the Bayesian algorithm, we obtained a normalized likelihood function on the binned linearized positions for each spiking-window." (Trajectory Score, p. e4)
- Windows: "We used fixed time windows of 256 ms for the detection of non-local positions during immobility. For the detection of trajectory replay we used windows with fixed number of spikes (i.e., spiking-window). The number of spikes contained in a window was selected for every session separately ... the minimum number of spikes that allowed us to decode the position of the animal with a decoding error of less than 30 cm." (Decoding Windows, p. e4)
- Event criterion: "Every four consecutive spiking-windows ... that passed the continuity (no jumps > 6 bins) and speed (absolute speed > 20 cm/s) criteria were analyzed" (p. e4). "Those four spiking-window events that had a unified trajectory score above the 95th percentile of their own shuffled distribution were selected", then extended window by window while the score stays significant (Trajectory Selection and Concatenation, p. e4).
- The "20 cm/s" is the speed of the DECODED trajectory, not the animal's speed.

Secondary SWR detection (for SWR co-occurrence, SWR power and SWR rate analyses only, not for choosing candidate replay events):
- "The local field potential signal (5 kHz) of one channel per HPC tetrode was subtracted with the signal of a reference channel that did not contain SWRs. For steps of 20 ms, 240 ms chunks of the signal were Fast-Fourier transformed and band-pass filtered between 150 and 250 Hz. The root mean square was then computed to obtain the ripple-band power for that channel. The mean ripple-band power was obtained by averaging over all channels. SWR events were detected by detecting periods where the ripple-band power was above 5 SD of the mean and the start and end times were also determined (1.5 SD)." (Oscillation Analyses, p. e5)
- "The rate of SWR events was computed for every trial by counting the number of SWR events during immobility periods" (p. e5): an analysis restriction.
- The period for the SD, and any minimum or maximum duration or merging, are not stated.

## Survey CSV discrepancies
- Detection Notes "windows were adjusted for number of spikes or cells": trajectory-replay detection used fixed-SPIKE windows ("For the detection of trajectory replay we used windows with fixed number of spikes", p. e4). The paper mentions a cell-count window only as a possible approach. Minor.
- Detection Notes "Decoded all immobility in 256 ms windows": the 256 ms windows were for NON-LOCAL POSITION detection. Trajectory replay used spike-count windows over the same immobility periods. The note conflates the two. Minor.
- "excluding trajectories under 20 cm/s" is right, but it refers to the decoded replay speed (">20 cm/s"), not the animal's.
- Other fields: Animal Speed 5 agrees (immobility < 5 cm/s). The SWR fields are N/A. The paper's secondary SWR detector (5 SD peak, 1.5 SD bounds, 150–250 Hz, mean over one channel per tetrode) is not recorded, which is consistent with Detection = Decoding.

Remaining deviations (secondary detector): the RMS window is 240 ms of FFT-band-passed signal in 20 ms steps, so its time resolution is much coarser than a Gaussian-smoothed Hilbert envelope. A boxcar sliding RMS can be computed in user code to match. Mean over channels is the same as Roumis up to a constant. Bounds at 1.5 SD, not the mean, so not Roumis_ripple_detector directly.
