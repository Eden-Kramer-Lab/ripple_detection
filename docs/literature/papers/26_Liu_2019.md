# Liu 2019 — Preconfigured patterns are the primary driver of offline multi-neuronal sequence replay
Source: the extracted text (pdftotext of the Dropbox PDF; Hippocampus 29:275–283, 2019; Liu, Sibille, Dragoi); title verified: yes
Trigger: MUA (silence-bounded spiking "frames" of sorted pyramidal cells; no rate threshold)

## Method as implemented
Detection criteria (Methods, p. 277–278, left column, txt lines 177–197):

- **State gate (detection):** "Spiking “frames” of activity were detected during slow-wave sleep periods in the sleep box (animal velocity < 1 cm/s and theta/delta ratio below 2, after Hilbert transform for 6–12 Hz for theta and 1–4 Hz for delta, and smooth with a 5 s Gaussian) and during awake resting period (animal velocity < 2 cm/s) at the ends of the linear track (Diba & Buzsaki, 2007; Dragoi & Tonegawa, 2013b)."
  - SWS: speed < 1 cm/s AND theta (6–12 Hz)/delta (1–4 Hz) ratio < 2 (Hilbert, 5 s Gaussian). No minimum SWS duration stated. The ratio definition is identical to Farooq 2019 Neuron (row 22).
  - Awake rest: speed < 2 cm/s at the track ends.
- **Event definition (silence-bounded):** "A spiking frame was defined as a transient increase in the multiunit firing activity of a population of at least four different pyramidal cells within a temporal window preceded and followed by at least 100 ms of silence that delimited the beginning and the end of the event (Dragoi & Tonegawa, 2011)." Results restate it: "epochs of transient increases in multiunit activity contributed by at least four pyramidal neurons flanked by at least 100 ms epochs of neuronal silence (Dragoi & Tonegawa, 2011)" (p. 278, right column, txt ~342–345).
  - No rate threshold, smoothing or z-score. "Multiunit" means the pooled spikes of sorted pyramidal cells: "Single cells were identified and isolated offline using the manual clustering program Xclust3 ... Pyramidal cells were distinguished from interneurons based on spike width, average rate, and autocorrelations" (txt 166–176).
  - Bounds: set by the >= 100 ms silences before and after.
- **Duration:** "Frames durations smaller than 80 ms or larger than 1.2 s were excluded from further analysis." Applies to the silence-bounded frame.
- **Participation:** >= 4 different pyramidal cells.
- Merging / artifact rejection: not stated.

Analysis-only items:
- **Ripple frames (analysis subset):** "Ripples were extracted from band-pass filtered (150–250 Hz) local field potential using Hilbert transform. Instantaneous power was computed and frames containing epochs above three standard deviations (SD) above the mean were detected and selected as ripple frames (Figure 2b,c)." Used for "ripple templates", compared with all-frame templates (R = 0.77). Channel, smoothing and normalization period: not stated.
- **Ripple-centered control epochs:** "we extracted epochs of 240 ms duration (i.e., the average duration length of the original frames) centered by the time of sharp wave ripple peaks occurring during sleep in the box and during awake rest sessions on the track." (txt 212–217). A control analysis ("data not shown" in Results).

## Inherited from
Liu cites **Dragoi & Tonegawa 2011** (Nature 469:397) for the frame definition. I read the PMC author manuscript (PMC3104398, BioC API text saved at a local copy). It defines events as "epochs composed of multiple individual spikes from at least four different place cells active on the novel arm or familiar track, separated by less than 50 ms and flanked by at least 50 ms of silence", during "sleep/rest (speed, <1 cm s−1) periods in the sleep box or awake rest (speed, <2 cm s−1) periods". So the cited paper used **50 ms** gaps/silence and place cells. Liu uses **100 ms** silence and pyramidal cells.

Liu also cites **Dragoi & Tonegawa 2013b** (PNAS; local PDF in Dropbox, text at a local copy) for the rest periods. That paper uses the 100 ms version: "epochs of multiunit activity recorded from at least six different pyramidal cells with a <100-ms interspike interval in the multiunit activity, flanked by epochs of >100 ms of silence", and in Methods "preceded and followed by at least 100 ms of silence that delimited the beginning and end of the event", with sleep speed < 1 cm/s. Its ripple detection (not used by Liu) was 120–200 Hz Hilbert amplitude, peak > 4 SD, bounds at 1 SD.
Conclusion: in this lineage the event is a cluster of pooled spikes whose inter-spike intervals are all < 100 ms, bounded by >= 100 ms gaps. Liu's own numbers (100 ms, >= 4 cells, 80 ms–1.2 s) are authoritative.

## Package mapping
Tier: B (borderline C: the package supplies only `segment_boolean_series`; the segmentation rule itself is user code)     Needs radiatum: n   Needs theta: y (theta/delta < 2 for SWS)   Needs sleep scoring: y (user code)

Recipe (`counts`: (n_time, n_pyramidal_units) at 1 kHz):
```python
import numpy as np, pandas as pd
from ripple_detection import segment_boolean_series, require_overlap

pop = counts.sum(axis=1)
silence = np.array(segment_boolean_series(pd.Series(pop == 0, index=time), minimum_duration=0.100)).reshape(-1, 2)
frames = np.column_stack([silence[:-1, 1], silence[1:, 0]])          # activity between consecutive >=100 ms silences
dur = frames[:, 1] - frames[:, 0]
n_cells = [(counts[(time >= s) & (time <= e)].sum(axis=0) > 0).sum() for s, e in frames]
frames = frames[(dur >= 0.080) & (dur <= 1.2) & (np.array(n_cells) >= 4)]
# restrict to SWS (speed < 1 & theta/delta < 2; user code) or to awake rest (speed < 2 at track ends)
# ripple frames: require_overlap(frames, ripples), ripples from a 150-250 Hz detector at 3 SD (see deviations)
```
(Smoke-tested on simulated spikes; it runs.)

Remaining deviations:
- The package has no silence/ISI segmentation. The recipe above is user code. Bin-based silence (>= 100 empty 1 ms bins) vs spike-time ISI >= 100 ms differ by up to one bin. Frame bounds here are the last/first silent samples, not the first/last spikes.
- Which pyramidal cells are pooled (all putative pyramidal vs place cells only) is not stated. The two cited papers differ.
- Ripple frames: the paper thresholds Hilbert *power* at 3 SD. `Kay_ripple_detector` / `Karlsson_ripple_detector` threshold the smoothed *envelope* (amplitude), and 3 SD of power is not 3 SD of amplitude. Channel choice and normalization period are unknown. Treat ripple frames as approximate.
- Ripple-centered 240 ms epochs need ripple peak times. `Zugaro_ripple_detector` and `Long_sharp_wave_ripple_detector` report `peak_time`, Kay/Karlsson do not, so the peak comes from user code on the envelope, then `peak ± 0.12 s`. No fixed-window option exists.
- SWS gate: user-computed theta/delta.
Smallest package addition (if C): a spike-cluster segmenter, e.g. `segment_by_silence(multiunit, time, minimum_silence=0.1, minimum_active_units=4, minimum_duration=0.08, maximum_duration=1.2)`. It would split the pooled spike train at every gap >= `minimum_silence` and return a detector-style DataFrame with `n_active_units`. That would make this tier A (apart from the state gate), and would also cover Dragoi & Tonegawa 2011/2013 and similar silence-bounded definitions.
