## Method as implemented

Main text (p. 676, txt ~103–108): "Candidate replay events were [detected as] surges in aggregate firing rate (30) during SWS epochs (fig. S11, A and C), which coincided with ripple events [fig. S11G; results remained unchanged when candidate events were restricted to ripples (fig. S12)]." Ref 30 = Davidson, Kloosterman & Wilson, Neuron 2009.

Detection criteria (SM, "Candidate replay events", SM p. 5–6):

- **State gate (detection): SWS by k-means on theta/delta.** "Candidate replay events were detected using place cell ensemble activity during slow wave sleep (SWS). SWS periods were first determined by k-means clustering of the theta (6–10 Hz) / delta (1–4 Hz) ratio computed from spectrograms during sleep sessions (min duration of SWS epoch >120 s, permitting brief discontinuities <1 s; Fig. S11A)." Fig. S11A legend: "K-means clustering identified epochs with the smallest ratio (min duration 120 s, brief interruptions <1 s)."
  - Ratio: spectrogram theta (6–10 Hz) power over delta (1–4 Hz) power. No fixed threshold: k-means (number of clusters not stated), and the cluster with the smallest ratio is SWS. SWS epochs > 120 s, gaps < 1 s bridged. Spectrogram window/step: not stated.
  - **Validation only (not a gate):** "The method was validated by comparing the outcome to episodes where the animal was immobile (linear velocity <0.05 cm/s for at least 120 s, with brief movements <1 s) and by visual examination of the video recordings."

- **Signal:** place cells only. "Pyramidal cells with at least one firing field were defined as place cells" (SM txt 778). Units are sorted (KlustaKwik/Klusters, Lratio < 0.05; SM txt 704–720).

- **Rate, smoothing, threshold, bounds:** "Candidate replay events were defined as epochs of elevated place cell spiking activity (30), i.e. when the instantaneous firing rate (1-ms bins, smoothed using a Gaussian kernel of s.d.= 10 ms) reached a peak >3 s.d. and remained greater than the mean (Fig. S11C)." Fig. S11C legend: "multi-unit activity (MUA) of place cells (time bin, 1 ms; black line, mean of the smoothed histogram, used to define the beginning and end of selected candidates; red line, 3 s.d. above the mean, used as a threshold for the peak)."
  - Peak > 3 SD; bounds at the mean; no sustained-duration requirement. The period for mean/SD is not stated; the Fig. S11C example is drawn on one SWS period (suggests SWS, inference).

- **Maximum duration:** "Candidate events lasting >500 ms were excluded." Minimum duration at detection: none stated.

- Speed criterion at detection: none (only the SWS gate). Merging: not stated.

Analysis-only restrictions:

- **Decoding:** "We only considered trajectory events involving at least three units in candidate events lasting >60 ms." (SM txt 861). The >= 3 units and > 60 ms apply to the events analyzed for trajectories, not to candidate detection.

- **Ripple detection (for Fig. S11G peri-event histograms and the Fig. S12 control):** "LFPs from all channels located in or close to the pyramidal layer were detrended and filtered between 100 and 250 Hz, then averaged. In order to prevent spurious detection due to high frequency noise contamination, the detrended signal from the selected channels was also filtered between 300 and 500 Hz, then averaged and subtracted from the mean filtered signal. Negative values, reflecting high frequency noise periods, were discarded while all positive values were z-scored, and ripples were defined as epochs during which the values remained above 1 s.d. with a peak greater than 3 s.d. Excessively brief (⩽ 20 ms) or long (⩾110 ms) epochs were excluded from subsequent analyses." (SM "Ripple detection", txt 1029–1038). Whether the "values" are squared/enveloped before subtraction: not stated. Smoothing: not stated.

- Fig. S12: "The analyses in Fig. 4 were replicated on candidate events restricted to ripple epochs." "Restricted to ripple epochs" could mean candidates overlapping a ripple, or candidates clipped to ripple bounds. Not specified.

**FMAToolbox / FindRipples question:** the SM says "All analyses were performed in Matlab ... using the Freely Moving Animal toolbox (FMAToolbox, M. Zugaro, http://fmatoolbox.sourceforge.net) and additional custom programs." (SM txt 741–743). `FindRipples` is **not named**. The ripple procedure described differs from FindRipples as the package implements it:

- channels are averaged, not squared-and-summed;

- a 300–500 Hz noise band is subtracted and negative values dropped;

- thresholds are 1/3 SD, not 2/5;

- durations are > 20 and < 110 ms, not 20–100 ms with a 30 ms merge;

- no moving-average smoothing is mentioned.
In any case ripples do not gate candidate detection here; they only enter a control analysis. The candidate rule is Davidson 2009's peak/mean rule with a 10 ms kernel.

## Inherited from

Ref 30, Davidson et al. 2009, for the "elevated activity" concept. Drieu restates every parameter it uses (10 ms SD vs Davidson's 15 ms; SWS vs Davidson's stopped periods < 5 cm/s; > 500 ms ceiling), so I did not follow the citation further. The package's `multiunit_HSE_detector` docstring already describes how to approximate the Davidson rule (`zscore_threshold=3.0, minimum_duration=0.0`).

## Analysis and interpretation

Each event is compared with 5000 independently circular-shifted posterior-column surrogates. The 20 ms windows are nonoverlapping; the published 4.5 cm place-field kernel is described as a width, without identifying it as SD.

## Uncertainties

FMAToolbox utilities support interpretation but do not supply a complete versioned replay caller. State selection, kernel-width interpretation and original runtime settings remain limited by the published description.

## Package mapping

Executable example: `drieu_2018` in [literature_recipes.py](../../../examples/literature_recipes.py). Its docstring records implementation choices and assumptions. Simulation checks establish that it runs; they do not establish equivalence to the authors’ original event set.
