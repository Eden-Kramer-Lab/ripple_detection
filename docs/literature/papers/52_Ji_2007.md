# Ji 2007 — Coordinated memory replay in the visual cortex and hippocampus during sleep
Source: the extracted text (pdftotext of the Dropbox PDF). The Supplementary Figs 1–12, Tables 1–3 and Supplementary Methods came from nature.com (MOESM1–16), converted with pdftotext. Supp. Fig. 12 holds the frame parameters. Title verified: yes (Ji & Wilson, Nat. Neurosci. 10:100–107).
Trigger: MUA ("frames": periods between population silences during SWS; these are up-state-scale periods, 0.1–3 s, not SWR-scale events)

## Method as implemented

Detection (hippocampal "frames"; the same procedure was run separately on visual cortex):
- Signal: "All multiunit spikes (not necessarily sorted single-unit spikes) from all tetrodes within the same recording area were used to determine frame boundaries" (Methods, "Frame definition", p. 106).
- Binning and smoothing: "Spikes from a recording area were combined and counted in 10 ms time bins. Spike counts were then smoothed using a gaussian window with σ = 30 ms." (p. 106). Supp. Fig. 12b gives the reason: "The smoothing was to prevent isolated spikes in silent periods from being classified as frames."
- Threshold (not a z-score): "Interframe silent periods were defined as periods with spike counts below a preset threshold, and frames as periods in between." (p. 106). How the threshold was set is in Supp. Fig. 12c: "The spike count threshold (T) was determined from distribution of spike counts with 10 ms bins ... The threshold (red vertical line) was defined as the spike count value at which the distribution reached its first minima." So T is a data-driven value of the smoothed pooled count per 10 ms bin.
- Bounds: the T crossings themselves. "The threshold crossing time points were assigned to either frame start times ... or end times" (Supp. Fig. 12b). There is no extension to a mean and no separate peak threshold.
- Merging: "consecutive frames with a gap shorter than a threshold were combined." (p. 106). The gap threshold G was set from the silent-period-duration distribution: "minimum frame gap (G) (red vertical line) was determined from distribution of silent period durations ... The peak at 10ms indicates short breaks within frames and the second peak ( > 100 ms) indicates peak duration of inter-frame silent periods." (Supp. Fig. 12d).
- Values per animal (Supp. Fig. 12): "animal 1, CTX: T = 0.8 , G = 80 ms; HP: T = 0.6, G = 90 ms. animal 2, CTX: T = 0.8, G = 70 ms; HP: T = 0.5, G = 70 ms. animal 3, CTX: T = 0.8, G = 80 ms; HP: T = 0.8, G = 90 ms. animal 4, CTX: T = 0.8, G = 70 ms; HP: T = 0.5, G = 80 ms".
- Minimum and maximum duration: not stated. The observed hippocampal frames were "mean 0.78 s, median 0.50 s", and cortical frames were "distributed widely between 0.1 and 3 s" (Results, p. 101).
- Brain state (detection domain): SWS. "EMG, hippocampal and cortical EEGs were used to classify sleep states at 1-s resolution into four stages: wake state, SWS, rapid-eye-movement sleep and an unspecified intermediate state ... SWS was characterized as having low EMG, high hippocampal ripple, low hippocampal theta and high cortical delta power48." (Methods, "Sleep stage classification", p. 106). Supp. Fig. 11 plots EMG power, cortical delta, hippocampal ripple power and the "power ratio of hippocampal EEG theta to delta". The numeric thresholds are not stated.
- Speed: none (sleep).
- Participation (analysis, not detection): "candidate frames, defined as those containing at least four active template cells" (Results, p. 103). The Supplementary Methods give the reason: frames with fewer than 4 cells cannot reach p < 0.05.
- Relation to ripples: "Ripples almost always appeared inside hippocampal frames ... Individual frames could contain none, one or multiple ripple events" (p. 102). A frame is therefore a container of zero or more ripples, not a ripple.

Separate ripple detection (analysis only, Fig. 2d and Supp. Fig. 1):
- "EEGs recorded from the CA1 pyramidal cell layer were first filtered for ripple band (80–250 Hz) ... Ripple events were detected using a threshold-crossing method on the filtered hippocampal EEG at ripple band7,30. Two thresholds were defined. If S is the standard deviation of an EEG trace, 3S was set as cross-threshold and 7S as peak-threshold. All the time points with absolute EEG values larger than the cross-threshold were identified. Time points separated by gaps smaller than 50 ms were grouped as a single event. Furthermore, only events with a peak absolute value larger than the peak-threshold were taken as ripple events" (Methods, "Frame-triggered EEG and ripple detection", p. 106).
- The measure is the rectified filtered signal against multiples of its SD (no mean subtraction is stated). Grouping at < 50 ms is applied before the peak test. The reference period for S and the channel choice are not stated.

## Survey CSV discrepancies
Row 52:
- MUA Z-score Thresh. "Adaptive": the threshold is not a z-score. It is a smoothed pooled spike count per 10 ms bin at the first minimum of its distribution (hippocampus T = 0.5–0.8). "Adaptive" is loosely right, and the notes ("spike count threshold determined empirically from distribution") are right.
- Combine Events Thresh. "#N/A": the paper does merge. Frames with gaps < G are joined, with G = 70–90 ms in hippocampus and 70–80 ms in cortex, per animal (Supp. Fig. 12).
- Min. Cells 4: this is the candidate-frame criterion for the replay test (analysis), not a detection criterion.
- MUA smooth 30 matches (Gaussian σ = 30 ms).
- Not recorded: SWS-only detection (sleep staged from EMG, hippocampal ripple, theta and cortical delta), and bounds at the threshold itself. Also, frames are 0.1–3 s events that contain ripples, so comparing their parameters with SWR detectors is not like-for-like.
- The SWR fields are #N/A, which is acceptable for the trigger. The paper does have an 80–250 Hz, 3S/7S, 50 ms-grouping ripple detector for analysis.

Smoke-tested on simulated multiunit binned to 10 ms (the first-minimum rule found T = 0.06 counts per bin and 153 frames on sparse simulated firing).
Remaining deviations:
- The threshold is data-driven and user code (first minimum of the smoothed-count histogram). `estimate_noise_threshold` implements Yu's mirrored-noise rule, not this one. The histogram binning is not stated, and the result depends on it.
- G is picked from the silent-duration histogram by eye or by user code. The paper reports only the chosen values.
- Frames are "periods in between" silent periods. `count >= T` treats a sample exactly at T as frame, where the paper's "below a preset threshold" also makes it frame. That matches.
- segment_boolean_series returns the first and last supra-threshold bin times. The paper's crossing times at 10 ms resolution differ by up to one bin.
- The package has no sleep staging. SWS (low EMG, high ripple power, low theta, high cortical delta, 1 s epochs) needs EMG and cortical EEG, which is user work. How frames are cut at SWS boundaries is not stated. exclude_overlap drops any frame touching non-SWS, and clipping is the alternative.
- Frames are up-state-scale (median 0.5 s). The package's duration and plausibility checks allow this, because `maximum_duration` is unset and `merge_close_events` has no ceiling by default.
- The secondary ripple detector is B: `filter_ripple_band(lfp, fs, band=(80, 250))`, then `x = abs(filtered) / filtered.std()`, then `segment_boolean_series(x > 3, 0.0)`, then `merge_close_events(runs, 0.050)`, then keep runs whose max(x) > 7 (user code). It is not Zugaro's detector, which squares, smooths and z-scores.
Smallest package addition (if C): not needed. An optional `threshold=` (absolute, non-z-scored) plus `bounds="threshold"` mode on a multiunit trace would make it A.
